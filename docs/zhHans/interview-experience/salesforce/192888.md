# Melee Combat Mechanic Design

## Design Overview

- Mechanic: Melee Attack and Dodge
- Design Type: Gameplay Mechanic Design
- Design Focus: Combat Loop, Player Decision-Making, Enemy Interaction

## Design Goals

The combat system is designed around the following goals:

### Responsive

Player actions should feel immediate and predictable.

### Accessible

The basic controls should be easy for new players to understand.

### Skill-Based

Combat effectiveness should depend on player timing and decision-making rather than only character statistics.

### Expandable

The system should provide a foundation that can later support additional mechanics such as different weapons, enemy types, combos, stamina, or special abilities.

## Core Gameplay Loop

The basic combat loop is:

**Observe → Position → Attack → React → Dodge → Reposition**

The player observes enemy behavior and chooses whether to attack or wait.

After initiating an attack, the player evaluates the enemy's response and decides whether to continue attacking, reposition, or dodge.

This creates a simple decision-making cycle rather than allowing the player to continuously attack without reacting to the enemy.

## Melee Attack

The melee attack is the primary offensive action.

### Basic Behavior

When the player presses the attack input:

1. The character enters an attack state.
2. An attack animation begins.
3. A hit detection window becomes active.
4. Enemies within the valid attack range receive damage.
5. The character returns to the normal combat state.

### Design Considerations

The attack system should communicate:

- Attack range
- Attack timing
- Hit confirmation
- Enemy reaction
- Recovery time

The attack should not be completely risk-free. The player should consider enemy positioning and attack timing before committing to an attack.

## Dodge

Dodge is the primary defensive action.

When the player activates Dodge:

1. The character enters a dodge state.
2. The character moves rapidly in the selected direction.
3. The player temporarily avoids incoming attacks.
4. The character returns to the normal combat state.

The dodge mechanic creates a direct relationship between enemy attack patterns and player movement.

A successful dodge allows the player to avoid damage and potentially create an opportunity for a counterattack.

## Attack and Dodge Relationship

The two primary mechanics create a simple risk-and-reward relationship.

### Attack

**Advantage:** Deals damage and progresses the encounter.

**Risk:** The player may be vulnerable during attack recovery.

### Dodge

**Advantage:** Avoids incoming damage and allows repositioning.

**Risk:** Using Dodge at the wrong time may leave the player out of position or unable to respond immediately.

The intended combat rhythm is therefore:

**Attack → Enemy Responds → Dodge → Reposition → Attack**

The player should continuously evaluate whether the current situation favors offense or defense.

## Enemy Interaction

Enemy attacks should provide clear visual and audio signals before they occur.

These signals allow players to learn enemy behavior and improve their timing.

A basic enemy attack sequence can be:

**Idle → Detect Player → Prepare Attack → Attack → Recovery**

The player's goal is to recognize the preparation phase and respond with an appropriate action.

This creates a readable relationship between enemy behavior and player input.

## User Stories

### User Story 1 — Basic Attack

**As a player, I want to perform a melee attack so that I can damage enemies and progress through combat encounters.**

### User Story 2 — Dodge

**As a player, I want to dodge incoming attacks so that I can avoid damage and reposition myself during combat.**

### User Story 3 — Timing

**As a player, I want to recognize enemy attack patterns so that I can choose the right moment to attack or dodge.**

### User Story 4 — Combat Feedback

**As a player, I want clear visual, audio, and animation feedback when my attacks connect so that I can understand the result of my actions.**

### User Story 5 — Skill Expression

**As a player, I want successful timing and positioning to improve my combat performance so that I feel rewarded for learning enemy behavior and improving my skills.**

## Basic State Structure

The system can be represented using the following simplified states:

### Idle

- Player can move.
- Player can initiate Attack or Dodge.

### Attacking

- Attack animation is active.
- Hit detection is enabled during a defined window.
- Player has limited control during the attack.

### Dodging

- Player moves in the selected direction.
- Player temporarily avoids incoming attacks.
- Player cannot initiate another action until the dodge state ends.

### Recovery

- Player returns to normal movement.
- Player can begin another action.

The simplified state flow is:

**Idle → Attack → Recovery → Idle**

or:

**Idle → Dodge → Recovery → Idle**

## Future Iteration

The initial system can be expanded through additional mechanics.

Potential future iterations include:

- Light and heavy attacks
- Attack combos
- Perfect dodge timing
- Counterattacks
- Stamina management
- Different weapon types
- Enemy-specific attack patterns
- Hit reactions
- Knockback
- Special abilities

These systems should only be introduced when they support the core combat experience.

The initial prototype should first validate whether the basic interaction between melee attacks and dodging feels responsive, readable, and satisfying.

## Success Criteria

The initial combat prototype can be evaluated based on the following questions:

1. Can players understand how to attack and dodge without extensive instruction?
2. Can players clearly identify when an enemy is about to attack?
3. Does successful dodging provide a meaningful advantage?
4. Does attacking require meaningful timing or positioning?
5. Do players feel that improved performance comes from learning enemy behavior?
6. Does the combat loop remain engaging across repeated encounters?

## Conclusion

The proposed combat system establishes a simple foundation based on two core actions: melee attack and dodge.

The primary design focus is the relationship between player decision-making and enemy behavior. By creating clear attack windows, readable enemy signals, and meaningful defensive timing, the system can provide a straightforward but skill-based combat experience.

The system is intentionally designed to be modular and expandable. Once the basic interaction is validated through prototyping and playtesting, additional mechanics can be introduced to increase depth without compromising the clarity of the core combat loop.
